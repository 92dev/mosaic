Read when: a human brief needs rewriting because the reader cannot follow its claim.
# Reader knowledge
The writer knows the steps that led to a conclusion; the reader may be missing several. An obvious step to the writer can still be unsupported for the reader.
Find where the reader's knowledge ends and explain the missing connections. Apply the brief's required form by reading `rule://human-gates`.
## Five recurring defects
1. **Metaphors used as terms:** words such as "seam", "lever", or "surface" can hide an unnamed function or relationship. Use the actual thing when that vocabulary is not shared.
2. **Concepts before introduction:** domain terms, code names, and labels invented during the work assume context the reader lacks. Supply the relationship needed to understand them.
3. **Nested sentences:** long dash chains, stacked parentheses, and noun piles make the reader hold several unfinished ideas. Separate the claims and connect them in order.
4. **Tables used to explain unknown concepts:** compressed cells can become a list of unanswered questions. Explain the needed relation first; retain tables when they make lookup or comparison clearer.
5. **Compression that removes reasoning steps:** shorter wording can omit why one fact supports the next. Restore the missing step without adding background the reader already knows.
## The correction cuts both ways
A domain expert may need this code's flow explained while already knowing the field's fundamentals. Teaching those fundamentals hides the actual change and can be condescending.
Treat terms the reader uses as known; questions reveal missing context, and "I know this" moves the starting point forward.
Let length follow the missing steps. Do not turn the examples into a global metaphor ban, a table ban, or a requirement to expand every reply.
